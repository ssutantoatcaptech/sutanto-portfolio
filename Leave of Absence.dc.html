Case study — Insurance

Modernizing the Leave of Absence Member Experience

A national insurance provider's disability, PFML and workplace absence programs, redesigned into one connected member experience — with AI-accelerated prototyping used to settle scope before a line of production code was written.

One case, one screen: the status tracker carries every outstanding obligation, its due date and its action — alongside coverage dates, uploaded documents and the next thing to do.

The problem

Members were managing a life event through a claims system built for adjudication.

Taking leave for a birth, a surgery or a family illness meant navigating disability, Paid Family Medical Leave and employer absence programs as three separate systems — each with its own request, its own status language, and its own idea of where the member was in the process.

The workflows were operationally complex and heavily regulated, so every simplification had to survive compliance review. Members fell back on phone calls; operations absorbed the cost. Self-service existed but few could complete a request inside it.

Approach
01 — Align on the real workflow

Worked alongside internal teams and client stakeholders to reconcile user needs, operational process and technical constraint into one agreed workflow — before design direction was set.

02 — Prototype to kill ambiguity

Leaned heavily on AI prototyping to produce interactive builds fast. Scope arguments resolved against something clickable, early, rather than against a requirements document.

03 — Connect the member journey

Leave requests, time tracking, status updates, payments and return-to-work designed as one continuous experience instead of five disconnected transactions.

04 — Build for the next product

Scalable UI patterns and an experience framework that carry across multiple insurance and absence products — a design-first foundation, not a one-release solution.
