Case study — Healthcare

Modernizing Operations: Clinical Quality & Intake Automation

Clinical case intake ran on spreadsheets against government compliance deadlines. I blueprinted the operation, found the automation worth building, and cut intake processing time by more than 85%.

client Major U.S. healthcare partner role Lead UX ArchitectService blueprinting · Workflow · UI system stakeholders Case intake managersMedical directorsPeer review committees outcome −85% intake time · 5/5 ease of use

Workflow transformation: the redesigned clinical quality interface, built around triage velocity and clear decision indicators for case managers.

The problem

A compliance deadline running on spreadsheets and manual reconciliation.

Case intake for AHRQ and HAC events, and the quality-issue reviews that followed, moved through spreadsheets and disconnected legacy databases. Clinical staff redacted records by hand and reconciled the same case in three places before review could begin.

The cost landed on the people least able to absorb it: medical directors and peer review committees spent their time on clerical correction rather than medical judgement, while government contract turnaround times slipped.

Approach
01 — Blueprint the operation

Service blueprinting reframed intake as one system rather than four teams, exposing where manual effort was redundant rather than protective.

02 — Find the triggers worth automating

Not everything should be automated. I prioritised the high-value triggers — validation, redaction, triage routing — and left clinical judgement human.

03 — Align the committees

Case intake managers, medical directors and peer review committees each held veto power. Process maps became the shared artifact that got approval for the future state.

04 — Test in two-week cycles

Lean UX iterations with real clinical users refined the workflows against the compliance calendar instead of a design timeline.

The work

Operational mapping: blueprinting the relationship between manual clinical review and automated data intake, to remove redundant administrative effort.

Friction analysis: pain points across the medical director and peer review lifecycle, ranked by automation value.

Intake efficiency: centralized validation replacing manual redaction and reconciliation across legacy databases.

High-value focus: automated data flow returns clinical staff to complex medical review instead of clerical entry.

Complexity visualization: the process maps that drove stakeholder alignment on the future-state architecture.
