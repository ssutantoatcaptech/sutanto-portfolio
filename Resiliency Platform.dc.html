Case study — Fintech

Untangling Resiliency: Bridging Observability and Reliability Gaps

An engineering resiliency platform served two audiences badly: the builder-fixers who repair systems at 3am and the executives who need to know the risk. I unified observability and reliability into one source of truth — and adoption more than doubled.

client Fortune 100 fintech — Operational Excellence role UX Strategy & Staff ArchitectResearch · System design · Data viz audiences SRE & platform engineersExecutive insight-seekers outcome 104% user growth · 86% retention

Vitality dashboards: unified health scoring that translates technical system logs into business value and operational risk.

The problem

Reporting was manual, siloed, and expensive enough that teams simply opted out.

Observability lived in one set of tools, application reliability in another. Engineers assembled resiliency reports by hand; executives received a version that had already gone stale. Neither group trusted the numbers enough to act on them.

Because the reporting burden fell on the teams being measured, coverage stalled at six high-priority applications. The platform's real problem wasn't missing data — it was that using it cost more than ignoring it.

Approach
01 — Sit with incident response

Empathy interviews with SRE leads, prioritising features against real incident-response workflows rather than a feature wishlist.

02 — Automate the reporting

Led the shift from manual, siloed reports to automated multi-persona dashboards, removing the cost that kept teams away.

03 — Standardise the patterns

One design language for dense data environments, reducing cognitive load during high-stress response and building platform trust.

04 — Score, then predict

A multi-metric scoring interface plus performance-theme visualizations, moving teams from reactive alerts to predictive trend mapping.

The work

Trend visualization: from static reactive alerts to predictive performance mapping, so SRE teams spot anomalies before customers do.

Service mapping: single points of failure surfaced inside a global microservices architecture.

Metric depth: drill-down from system health to log-level detail without leaving the interface.

Unified resiliency: the scoring framework and design language that align engineering effort with business risk and compliance standards.

Outcome

104%

Growth in unique active users

86%

Platform retention rate

6 → 35

Apps under risk remediation

Growth came from a design-led adoption strategy, not a mandate: once reporting was automatic and the patterns were predictable, teams brought their own applications to the platform. Critical risk coverage expanded nearly six-fold by making the reporting burden disappear.
